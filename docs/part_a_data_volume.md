**The "shock number".**
- One 100 Hz vibration sensor on one machine produces **3.15 billion readings a year**. That is ten times all 10 slow sensors on that machine, and as much as the 1 Hz streams of **ten whole machines**.
- Fitting one to each of the 100 machines gives **315 billion readings a year, ten times the whole 1 Hz fleet**.
- The real Bosch accelerometers sample at **2 kHz on 3 axes**. Streaming that raw from 100 machines would mean **18.9 trillion values a year** (figure `part_a_volume_per_year.png`).

## A.3 Storage and bandwidth

**Byte-size assumptions:**
- **Binary record:** 8 B timestamp (int64) + 8 B value (float64) + 2 B machine ID + 2 B sensor ID = **20 B**.
- **Value-only:** 8 B, with timestamps implicit at a fixed rate.
- **JSON over MQTT**, sizes **measured** by serialising realistic messages:
  - one value per message = 125 B payload, 219 B on the wire including topic, MQTT header and TCP/IP;
  - 10 sensors batched per second = 38 B per value;
  - a 100 Hz one-second block = 8.25 B per value.

| Scenario (20 B binary record) | Bandwidth | Per day | Per year |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Fleet 1 Hz (100 × 10 × 1 Hz) | 0.16 Mbps | 1.73 GB | 0.63 TB |
| One 100 Hz sensor | 0.016 Mbps | 0.17 GB | 0.06 TB |
| Fleet 100 Hz (100 × 1 × 100 Hz) | 1.60 Mbps | 17.3 GB | 6.31 TB |
| Design point (10 × 1 Hz + 1 × 100 Hz per machine) | 1.76 Mbps | 19.0 GB | 6.94 TB |
| Raw 2 kHz × 3 axes on 100 machines | **96 Mbps** | **1,037 GB** | **378 TB** |

**Encoding matters as much as the sample rate.** With one JSON message per reading, the 1 Hz fleet alone needs 1.75 Mbps, 11× the binary figure, and the raw 2 kHz case exceeds 1 Gbps (figure `part_a_bandwidth_by_encoding.png`).

## A.4 Measured compression (real data, not assumed)

Each dataset was written to disk in six formats with pyarrow (figure `part_a_measured_compression.png`):

| Format | UMich CNC 10 Hz, 48 signals (REAL) | Bosch 2 kHz vibration (REAL) | Azure PdM hourly (BENCHMARK) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CSV (baseline) | 6.5 MB = 1.0× | 285 MB = 1.0× | 77.5 MB = 1.0× |
| CSV + gzip | 4.7× | 4.1× | 2.4× |
| Parquet, uncompressed | 4.8× | 2.8× | 2.5× |
| Parquet + Snappy | 5.8× | 3.3× | 2.5× |
| **Parquet + Zstd (level 3)** | **6.9×** | **5.0×** | **2.7×** |
| Parquet + Zstd (level 9) | 7.1× | 5.0× | 2.7× |

**Reading:**
- Slow, structured CNC signals compress about 7×, because columns repeat values and change smoothly.
- Raw vibration compresses only about 5× against CSV, and barely beyond its own binary size. **Vibration is mostly broadband noise, and lossless compression cannot remove noise.**
- That is why feature extraction, not compression, is the right tool for high-frequency data.

## A.5 Edge feature extraction on real data (key result)

**What was done.**
- All **1,702** Bosch files were processed: 24.8 h of real 2 kHz tri-axial data, 178.6 M samples per axis.
- Each was cut into **88,536 one-second windows**.
- Per axis and window: mean, RMS, peak, peak-to-peak, crest factor, kurtosis, skewness, five FFT band-energy fractions, dominant frequency and spectral centroid. That is **42 values per second instead of 6,000 samples**.

| Result | Value |
|---|---|
| Reduction per machine-second | 24,000 B → 168 B = **143×** |
| Measured on the full dataset | **120×** vs raw float32; **53×** vs the compressed HDF5 files on disk |
| Fleet uplink (100 machines) | **19.2 Mbps → 0.13 Mbps** |
| Fleet storage per year | **75.7 TB → 0.53 TB** |
| Extraction speed | 40 s for 24.8 h of data on a laptop CPU, about 2,200× faster than real time |
| Is the fault signal kept? | Yes. Single features separate good from bad operations: ROC-AUC up to **0.82** for the Y-axis 150–300 Hz band. An Isolation Forest trained only on good operations reaches **0.98**, and **0.98** when trained on 2019 data and tested on 2021. |

## A.6 Discussion

**Why high-frequency vibration creates storage and transmission challenges.**
- Vibration has to be sampled at 2–10× the highest frequency of interest (Nyquist). Bearing and tool faults show up between 100 Hz and several kHz, which forces kHz rates.
- **Volume:** a single 100 Hz channel on every machine generates ten times the readings of the entire 1 Hz fleet. At the real 2 kHz × 3 axes it is 600,000 values/s for 100 machines.
- **Bandwidth:** 96 Mbps sustained would take a plant's whole internet link, which is typically 50–100 Mbps of leased line shared with ERP, video and office traffic.
- **Storage:** 378 TB/year raw, and noise does not compress (A.4).
- **Cost:** per-message cloud pricing explodes when each reading is its own message. ₹14–30 lakh/month against ₹0.4–0.8 lakh batched (`cost_model.md`).
- **Ingestion and processing:** time-series databases and dashboards slow down at billions of rows. Most of those rows are noise that nobody queries.

**Why raw high-frequency data should not always go to the cloud.**
- The decision-relevant information is far smaller than the signal itself. RMS, kurtosis and band energies carry the fault signature (AUC 0.98 here), so shipping raw data pays 143× more for very little extra information.
- Protective actions (spindle stop, high-vibration alarm) cannot wait for a WAN round-trip, or for an internet outage to end.
- Raw OT data leaving the plant enlarges the attack surface and the data-residency scope.
- Raw data is still useful **occasionally**: for root-cause analysis and for training new models. Keep a short ring buffer at the edge and upload raw windows on demand, for example the 10 s around an anomaly.

**The role of edge processing.**
- The edge gateway sits beside the machines (Purdue L3). It converts protocols, filters noise, checks data quality, extracts features, batches messages, runs alarms and local inference, and buffers data while the WAN is down.
- In this project's MQTT demo, the gateway's outbox delivered all 482 messages through a subscriber outage and a WAN outage, with 0 lost and 0 duplicated.

**Compression, aggregation and feature extraction: three different tools.**

| Technique | What it keeps | Typical gain (measured here) | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lossless compression (Parquet+Zstd, gzip) | Every bit | 5–7× (2.7× for already-aggregated data) | Archives, the data lake |
| Aggregation / downsampling (1-min mean/min/max, 1 Hz) | Trends, levels | 60× for 1 Hz → 1 min | Dashboards, OEE, slow sensors |
| Batching (one message per machine-second) | Every value; fewer messages | 35–70× lower ingestion cost | All telemetry |
| Event-based / report-by-exception (send on change > deadband) | Changes only | Large for slow-changing signals (status, temperature) | Status, setpoints, alarms |
| **Feature extraction** (RMS, kurtosis, FFT bands, crest factor) | The signal's diagnostic content | **143×** | High-frequency vibration, current signatures |

**Combined design for this factory:**
- 1 Hz sensors are batched per machine-second and sent as is.
- Status and alarms are sent by exception.
- Vibration becomes 42 features per second, with raw windows kept for 7 days at the edge.
- Everything is stored as Parquet+Zstd in the lake.
- Result: about 0.69 Mbps for the fleet and 0.4 TB per year of compressed history.
